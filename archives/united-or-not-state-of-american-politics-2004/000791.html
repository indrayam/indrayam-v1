I wrote my first blog entry on “US politics” exactly 3 months back on Aug. 3rd, 2004. As some may know by reading my blog entries, I gave this Election my time, my money, heck practically my life during these past three months. I would be lying if I said that I am not upset tonight. Infact, I am extremely disappointed and feel a sense of personal failure. But I have no regrets. Well, maybe some..;-)

I am sure I will have a clearer head tomorrow or a week from now, but as I see it today, here’s a list of things that worries me:

1. Faith/Values/Evangelicals/Catholics/Religion: This gradual emergence and impact of discussion of “Faith” or “Religion” or “Moral Values” in US Politics is HUGE and I don’t see this abating anytime soon. President Bush and his Republican friends will run with this apparently unbeatable “cocktail” as long as citizens of states like Idaho, Montana, Kansas and Alabama allow them to. Personally, I hate it. I hate this aspect of US politics more than anything else.

2. Media: The fact that the voters of this country are so misinformed about the happenings in their own country, let alone the world, is another huge problem that will threaten the very fabric of US democracy. A “free” media does not automatically guarantee a “fair and balanced” media as we’ve seen it again and again, thanks to our friends in 24×7 Cable News Business or Radio Shows.

3. Fate of Democrats/Liberals: I used to always tell my friends and/or relatives that if Democrats can’t win this time, I don’t know when they ever will. With tonight’s defeat (both in “Electoral” and “Popular” Vote) will set the Democrats back by at least a good 5 to 10 years, unless they figure out a way to better “define” themselves, their “ideologies” (if any) and their “leadership”.

I am sure it’s not the end of the world, although it sure feels like it..:-)

One can only “hope” that better sense will prevail in President Bush’s style of leadership (replacing “arrogance” with “pride”) and his Administration’s policies (be more “pro-life” than “pro-war”).

Though I share with you a profound disappointment for the results, I don’t see why you should feel a sense of personal failure. I’m not American, though I have lived in the US for 5 years (not living anymore), and am thinking about going back. I remember that I had a British English teacher in my homeland, and she was always criticizing my country. Every class she would say something negative in regards the criminality, the air, traditions, and, of course, politics. I always wanted to ask her to leave my country and go back to hers. As a result, as an immigrant, I’m always reluctant to criticize anything about the country that I’m currently living. However, not only you did a great job, but also made your point clearly, without ever criticizing AMERICA as a whole, just parts that you didn’t agree. Congratulations and a big thank-you!

Now, I agree with your faith and politics views. As I wrote before, I’m a Christian but I don’t see why faith and politics should mix. People that in one day are trying to go on with their daily routines are dead in the other. And waht for?
