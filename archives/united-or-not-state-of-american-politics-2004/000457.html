Before we start dissecting the data shown above, let’s get the terminology straight:

VAP - Voting Age Population (basically, Americans eligible to vote)

Registered - Registered voters

Actual Vote - Total number of voters who “actually” voted on the day of the election

% TO VAP - (Actual Vote/VAP)*100

% TO REG - (Actual Vote/Registered)*100

With that out of the way, let’s see what this table is really saying:

1. In 2000, almost 50 million registered voters did not vote on the day of the elections!

2. If you look at the data for 2004, I literally tried extrapolating the values for VAP and Registered Voters by looking at the trends of the previous elections. So, how did I come up with 132 million actual voters on Nov. 2nd, 2004? Well, if you look at 1964 and 1968, you will notice that given the right political climate (like JFK’s assassination or Vietnam war), registered American voters tend to exercise their voting rights in droves. A similar conclusion can be drawn upon looking at the data for the last 3 elections. Notice that the % TO REG value was highest in 1992 probably because Americans wanted to come out and vote for or against the first Iraq war and the sluggish economy.

9/11, Iraq war and the Economy (not to mention the deeply divided political climate of this country) leads me to believe that about 80% of the registered voters will come out on Nov. 2nd to vote! It might be a conservative estimate, but if we went with 80%, that would mean there will be a total of 132 million approx. Americans who would be voting this Fall. That is 27 million new voters compared to 2000. Considering that the previous election was decided on 537 votes only, there is ample lot of room for both Democrats and Republicans to dream!

As I had alluded to in the past, President Bush cannot win this election by banking on die-hard Republicans who have diligently voted for Republican candidates in the past. He will need spanking new “Republican” voters from this pool of 27-odd million and a majority of them to safely clear the hurdle of getting more “popular” and “electoral” vote this time around. As for Senator Kerry, all he can hope to achieve is to make sure that he can hold his ground against the continuing “negative” onslaughts that is bound to increase in the coming weeks and months leading up to the elections.
