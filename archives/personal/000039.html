There are IT professionals who have no clue what a blog is..

Posted : May 3, 2004 at 8:27 pm [America/Los_Angeles]

[via Blogging Roller]
Personally, blogging comes very, very close to fulfilling one of the many dreams that folks had when WWW hit the scene. That “everyone would or could be an author or a publisher in their own right“. Well, that dream really has only begun to materialize thanks to blogging and the tools that support the easy “production” or “publication” of content (Blogging tools and Blogging engines) and the tools that make it easier to “mine” or “consume” the information produced (like the many News Aggregators). So, I find it really hard to fathom that there are IT professionals (pretty sincere ones too, as I might hastily add) who apparently have no clue what it is.

To all rest of the blogging gents and ladies out there who blog or at least make a sincere attempt at doing it, you might enjoy reading Tim Bray’s post titled Sun Policy on Public Disclosure. Here’s a quote:

Many of us at Sun are doing work that could change the world. We need to do a better job of telling the world. As of now, you are encouraged to tell the world about your work, without asking permission first (but please do read and follow the advice in this note). Blogging is a good way to do this.
…
If you start writing, remember the Web is all about links; when you see something interesting and relevant, link to it; you’ll be doing your readers a service, and you’ll also generate links back to you; a win-win.
…
Another way to be interesting is to expose your personality; almost all of the successful bloggers write about themselves, about families or movies or books or games; or they post pictures.

Well, it’s a little difficult to comment on how to start blogging. But here are some pointers:

  1. I first dabbled with a few blogging engines (especially, MT and Roller) on a server in our intranet. There are many, many more options in your specific language of choice. For example, Java-based blogging engines include Roller, Blojsom and Pebble.
  2. Once you have it set up, play with it’s customizations and internals just to get a hang of the software
  3. When you’re ready, unleash yourself on to the Internet

If you’re one of those who really does not have the time or bandwidth to do all this, try enrolling with some of the free BSPs (Blog Service Providers) like JRoller or Live Journal. I am sure there are many others, but those are the more popular ones in my opinion.

Once you’ve a place where you can blog, look for a blogging client (like w.bloggar) which makes it super easy for you to post.

Oh, and once you’re really blogging, come back and read the article by Tim Bray referenced here. It’s very well written indeed.
HTH
