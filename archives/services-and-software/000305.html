Things that I DO NOT like about Gmail in its current avataar

Posted : June 22, 2004 at 3:30 pm [America/Los_Angeles]

Don’t get me wrong: Gmail rocks! Google continues to amaze me with their uncanny ability of creating web applications that have a simple UI, are extremely functional and perform exceptionally well. I also understand that Gmail is currently in BETA. However, I thought I will take a minute to share two things that I don’t like about it in its current avataar:

  1. I hate that their is no way to know that I have a new email! I am not sure how they would pull this off, since the only Google software that continuously resides in my memory is the Google toolbar (provided I am using IE and I keep it open all the time).
  2. In Gmail, Google has replaced the concept of folders with Labels. The Gmail Help has this to say about it:

    We’ve replaced them (folders) with something better — labels. Labels provide all the functionality of folders and give you an extra bonus: you can add more than one label to a conversation. That’s something you can’t do with folders. Once you’ve created a label, you can view all the messages with that label by searching or by clicking the label.

    I guess the name “Label” is the thing that really throws me off. If you’ve used Thunderbird or other rich email clients, you know that Label is something that you use to flag a message as “Important, To do, etc.”. Also, the fact that adding a label to a bunch of emails does not remove it from the Inbox, as would be the case if you moved an email to a folder, is a pain. In Gmail, the traditional behavior of moving emails to a folder is a two step process: First, select your emails and apply labels appropriately. Then, click on “Archive” for the mails to disappear from your Inbox. I have a feeling I would end up with labels that look like folder names (family, personal, linux-list, etc.) as well as traditional label names (Important, to-do, etc. etc.).

Wonder what’s so terrible about supporting folders and labels, as most email clients seem to do.

- Anand
