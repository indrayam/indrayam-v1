Domains moved to GoDaddy!

Posted : January 3, 2005 at 12:04 pm [America/Los_Angeles]

This morning I got an email from GoDaddy confirming that my domains (indrayam.com and drishtikona.com) were transferred to them. In other words, it took less than 48 hrs. for the entire transfer to occur. Not bad. Not bad at all.

Anyway, so here’s what I plan on doing today:

1. Unlock my domains at GoDaddy. By default, they’re transferred in a locked status meaning that I cannot update anything on my domains. What do I need to edit? The Primary and Secondary Nameserver entries for my domains since I am also changing my DNS Hosting Provider to ZoneEdit (free for first 5 zones) from EasyDNS.

2. Create 2 new “zones” in ZoneEdit for my two domains (indrayam.com and drishtikona.com). ZoneEdit will provide me with two Nameservers for each of my domains. These two will act as the “new” Primary and Secondary DNS entries for my domains.

3. Update GoDaddy with the new ZoneEdit Nameservers for each of the two domains (see figure above).

4. Wait for 48 hrs (as recommended by GoDaddy, although I think it propogates fairly quickly) and then use the DNS Query Tool dig to make sure that ZoneEdit Nameservers are indeed the new Nameservers for the two domains.

5. Delete the entries for indrayam.com and drishtikona.com in EasyDNS.

6. That’s it!

Now, since I am not changing IP Address of either of the two domains, I have a feeling that the change will be completely transparent to the end users and there will be NO downtime.

Starting with Step 2 (as shown above) NOW…

David:

Apologize for the procrastination in getting back to you David. Frankly, I am having a bit of a difficulty in understanding this line of yours:

“Does ZoneEdit provide a static IP”

ZoneEdit only offers DNS services as far as I know. However, you can get a static IP from your Broadband provider, provided they offer it. SBC (DSL provider) here in CA does, but Comcast (Cable provider) I think does not. You will have to dig this one up.

Now, as for “..wanting to host my site on my own server..”, well, as you must have seen from my post a few days back, you can use a little DynDNS and Port forwarding magic to host a website at your home (http://indrayam.homeip.net). Now, having said that, I would not recommend that you host a “production” quality site on a dynamic IP. So, here’s what you need to check:

1. Check out if your DSL provider offers static IP and that it’s not too costly
2. Check your default “upstream” speed by going to DSLReports Speed Test site (http://www.dslreports.com/stest) and if your Upstream speed is > 300 kbps range, I think you’re doing pretty good.

That’s it. If #1 and #2 work out, you’re ready to start working on hosting a website at home. If not, you can always host non-critical stuff at home using DynDNS approach.
